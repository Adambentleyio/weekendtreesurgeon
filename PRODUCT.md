# Product

## Register

brand

## Users

Homeowners in Cheshire (Nantwich, Crewe, Hankelow, Audlem and surrounding villages) who need small tree surgery, hedge cutting, or garden clearance. They arrive comparing options but the deciding factor is trust: they want a local tradesman who is personable, reliable, and cares about doing the job right. They are wary of the untrustworthy tradesman stereotype and are looking for signals that this is a real person who takes pride in his work and values his community.

Their context: browsing on a phone while the kids are in the bath, or on a laptop after dinner. They need to quickly see who Adam is, what he does, where he covers, and how to reach him, without feeling marketed to.

## Product Purpose

Communicate that Adam Bentley is a trustworthy, skilled tree surgeon offering small local jobs at fair prices. The website exists to convert browsing homeowners into inquiries by establishing Adam as a real person — warm, honest, grounded — who will treat their property and their trust with care. Success is measured by contact form submissions, phone calls, and word-of-mouth referrals driven by the site.

## Brand Personality

Warm, honest, grounded. The site should feel like a recommendation from a neighbor, not a corporate pitch. Adam's voice is straightforward and personable — a father of two who takes integrity and service seriously, not a salesman. The emotional goal is calm confidence: visitors should leave thinking "he seems like a good guy, I'll give him a call."

## Anti-references

- **The "Industrial Logger":** Buzzsaws, stark black/orange warning colors, heavy machinery graphics. Looks destructive.
- **The "Cowboy Lopper":** Workers without PPE, messy job sites, loose truck loads. Looks unsafe and uninsured.
- **The "Silicon Valley Tech":** Neon gradients, dark mode as default, sharp abstract shapes, robotic corporate language. Looks cold.
- **The "Elite Architect":** Ultra-minimalist white space, high-end luxury garden layouts. Looks too expensive and pretentious.
- **The generic tradesman template:** Stock photos of guys in hard hats, corporate trust badges, blue-and-white sanitized layouts. Feels interchangeable and impersonal.

## Design Principles

1. **Show the real person.** Adam's face, Adam's words, Adam's values. Real project photos over stock imagery. The site is a handshake, not a brochure.

2. **Trust through transparency.** Make it effortless to see who he is, what he does, and why he's different. No hidden pricing drama — just clarity and honesty.

3. **Local, not corporate.** The site should feel rooted in Cheshire. Like a neighbor's recommendation, not a franchise. Community over scale.

4. **Safety without the hard-hat aesthetic.** Communicate professionalism, insurance, and proper equipment without the industrial warning-sign look. Warm and capable, not cold and institutional.

5. **Warmth over polish.** The site should feel like a conversation with someone you trust. Approachable language, genuine photos, no marketing-funnel slickness.

## Accessibility & Inclusion

Aim for WCAG 2.2 AAA wherever achievable. Specific considerations:
- All images must have meaningful alt text
- Color contrast ratios must meet AAA minimums (7:1 for normal text, 4.5:1 for large text)
- Respect `prefers-reduced-motion` — no auto-playing or motion-heavy animations
- Test with color blindness simulators; do not rely on color alone to convey information
- Keyboard-navigable with visible focus indicators
- Content readable at 200% zoom without horizontal scroll
